# 1. Overview

AMS-02 has been operating on the ISS since **May 2011**, collecting more than **200 billion cosmic-ray events** in 11 years.  
The experiment provides **collider-level precision** measurements across leptons, antiprotons, and nuclei up to TeV energies.

Main physics goals (highlighted in PDF):

- Search for **antimatter and dark matter** signatures  
- Measure CR nuclei from **Z = 1 to Z = 26**  
- Study **time evolution** and solar modulation of cosmic-ray fluxes  

---

# 2. The AMS-02 Instrument

AMS-02 combines a 0.14 T permanent magnet with 5 major subdetectors that provide **redundant measurements**:

- **Silicon Tracker (L1–L9)**:  
  - 5–10 μm precision  
  - Rigidity reach: ~2 TV for Z=1, ~3 TV for Z>1  
- **TRD**: electron/positron vs proton separation  
- **TOF**: β measurement, direction discrimination  
- **RICH**: precise velocity → isotope separation  
- **ECAL**: 17 X₀ calorimeter with 3D shower imaging and ~10⁶ proton rejection  

Additional detail from the PDF:  
AMS has **300,000 readout channels** and **650 microprocessors**, broadcasting data at ~10 Mbit/s.

# 3. Physics Highlights

## 3.1 Leptons (e⁺ and e⁻)

AMS provides:

- **e⁻ spectrum** up to **1.9 TeV**  
- **e⁺ spectrum** up to **1.0 TeV**  

### Key discoveries:

- **High-energy positron excess** cannot be explained by secondary production alone.  
- A **cutoff near ~800 GeV** appears in the positron spectrum.  
- Electrons show **no such cutoff** up to 1.9 TeV.  

This suggests **additional sources** such as:

- Pulsar Wind Nebulae (PWNe)  
- Supernova remnants (SNRs)  
- Dark matter annihilation  

---

## 3.2 Antiprotons (p̄)

AMS measured p̄ over **1–500 GV**, drastically improving precision over previous missions.

### Main observation:

- **p̄/p ratio flattens** at high energies  
  → at the limit of pure secondary-model predictions  
  → consistent with some dark matter models, but also astrophysical uncertainties

---

## 3.3 Nuclei (Z = 1–26)

AMS discovered that CR nuclei fall into **three distinct spectral groups** (PDF Figure 3):

1. **Primary groups:** He–C–O–Fe and Ne–Mg–Si  
2. **Secondary group:** Li–Be–B, F  
3. **Mixed group:** N–Na–Al  

### Additional major result:

- **Spectral hardening above ~200 GV**  
  observed across nearly all species

This behavior challenges simple propagation models and indicates complexity in CR acceleration/transport.

# 4. Time-Dependent Cosmic Ray Fluxes

AMS provides the **first daily rigidity-resolved flux measurements** across:

- Protons  
- Helium  
- Electrons/positrons  
- Nuclei  

This covers the entire:

- **Rise of Solar Cycle 24**  
- **Solar maximum (~2014)**  
- **Decline to solar minimum (~2019)**  

### Discoveries (PDF Figure 4):

- **27-day, 13.5-day, 9-day periodicities** due to solar rotation  
- **Hysteresis in He/p ratio**  
  - He responds differently before/after solar maximum  
  - 7σ effect below 2.4 GV  
- **Charge-sign–dependent effects** in e⁺/e⁻ after the 2014 polarity reversal  

These results show how precisely AMS can probe heliospheric structure.

---

# 5. Long-Term Outlook

AMS-02 is expected to run **through at least 2030**, and possibly until ~2034–2035.  
This could cover a **full 22-year solar magnetic cycle**.

Long-term impact:

- High-statistics CR spectra to constrain propagation and sources  
- Improved dark-matter sensitivity (antiprotons, antihelium)  
- Daily, monthly, yearly solar-modulation datasets unmatched by any other mission
